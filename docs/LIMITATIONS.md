# Scientific & Operational Limitations

This document outlines the strict scientific boundaries, numerical assumptions, and operational constraints of the Dam Break Decision Support System.

---

## 1. Unverified Sample Rasters & Provenance Caveats

1. **Unknown Raster Origin**:
   The flood inundation rasters (`hidkal_depth.tif`, `hidkal_velocity.tif`, `hidkal_arrival.tif`) in `data/raw/data_hidkal/` are **unverified sample rasters of unknown provenance**. They are provided exclusively for pipeline validation and GUI demonstration. They must **never** be interpreted as verified hydrodynamic simulations, certified engineering dam-break hazard maps, or official government evacuation zones.
2. **Elevation & Vertical Datum Uncertainties**:
   In `hidkal_dem.tif`, numeric values span 600.0001 to 682.5. The physical elevation unit (meters vs feet) and vertical reference datum (e.g., EGM96, WGS84 ellipsoidal height, or Survey of India Mean Sea Level) are unverified.
3. **NoData Encoding Discrepancies**:
   In `hidkal_arrival.tif`, the header lists `GDAL_NODATA = "-9999"`, but dry/unflooded cells in the array contain `+9999.0` (294,000 cells). The system applies dual-masking (`val <= 0`, `val >= 9990`, `NaN`) to prevent false arrival spikes, but the raw file remains unchanged.
4. **Non-Square Pixel Spacing**:
   Grid spacing in EPSG:4326 is `0.00040000°` longitude (~44.4 m) by `0.00033333°` latitude (~36.9 m). Metric spatial calculations must project to UTM Zone 43N (EPSG:32643) to prevent anisotropic spatial distortion.

---

## 2. Infrastructure & Exposure Screening Limitations

1. **Representative Point-Sampling**:
   Physical asset exposure is evaluated via centroid / interior point sampling against unverified depth grids. Building sub-structure elevation, plinth height, multi-story structural resistance, boundary wall protection, and local micro-topography are not modeled.
2. **Road Centerline Screening**:
   Road vulnerability is evaluated along line midpoints. Dynamic bridge scour, culvert choking, embankment failure, and transient flood velocity forces are not assessed.
3. **OpenStreetMap Incompleteness**:
   OSM vector data reflects open-source community contributions and may not represent complete, up-to-date cadastral surveys, newly constructed infrastructure, or official administrative assets.

---

## 3. Illustrative Damage & Loss Assumptions

1. **Illustrative Parametric Valuations**:
   The depth-damage curves and replacement costs (e.g., ₹50,00,000 for critical infrastructure, ₹15,00,000 for residential buildings) are **illustrative user-editable baseline assumptions**.
2. **Non-Certified Economic Models**:
   Direct monetary loss numbers generated by the tool do not represent actuarial appraisals, insured loss certifications, or official disaster relief compensation assessments. Mandatory acknowledgement of unverified inputs is strictly required before calculation.

---

## 4. Preliminary Route Screening Constraints

1. **Topological Feasibility Only**:
   Route calculations use NetworkX Dijkstra shortest-path logic on the OSM road graph, avoiding edges where sample raster depth > 0.
2. **Operational Evacuation Disclaimers**:
   The calculated path is a **preliminary topological screening** and does NOT guarantee real-world evacuation safety. Live traffic congestion, emergency vehicle clearance, physical road blockages, fallen power lines, flash debris flows, and structural bridge collapse are NOT validated.

---

## 5. Hydrodynamic Solver & Engine Gating

1. **Delft3D Flexible Mesh / D-Flow FM**:
   HydroMT generates valid model input templates (`.ext`, `.mdu`, `.dimr`), but the numerical shallow-water solver (D-Flow FM) requires local installation of licensed Deltares software and calibrated boundary hydrographs. Solver execution is strictly gated (`ENABLE_DFLOWFM_EXECUTION=false`).
2. **PySPH Lagrangian Particle Solver**:
   PySPH is designed for laboratory-scale free-surface column collapse benchmarks (< 10 m domain). Computational particle limits ($O(N \cdot k)$ neighbor search) prevent regional basin-scale flood routing (> 25 km). Solver execution is gated (`ENABLE_PYSPH_EXECUTION=false`).
3. **Multi-Engine Spatial Comparison**:
   Comparison metrics (extent IoU, CSI, MAE, RMSE) require genuine completed simulations with verified physical units. Synthetic or unverified sample rasters are never assigned to either engine.

---

## 6. Google Earth Engine Connector Limitations

1. **Candidate Observations Only**:
   Satellite backscatter changes (Sentinel-1 SAR) or optical indices represent candidate water-change observations, not ground-truth inundation depth or flow velocity. Radar speckle, canopy penetration limits, and wind surface roughening affect backscatter interpretation.
2. **Authentication & Task Gating**:
   The GEE connector operates in dry-run mode until configured with Google Cloud Application Default Credentials (ADC). Background cloud export tasks are disabled by default (`ENABLE_GEE_TASKS=false`) to protect against unintended cloud billing.

## 7. ANUGA Regional Pilot & Refined Adaptive Model (Phase 15, 16 & 17)

1. **Scientific Status**:
   *Hypothetical refined ANUGA pilot — not a forecast or validated Hidkal prediction.* Vertical datum and DEM elevation units remain assumed based on source interpretation. Physical assumptions (breach opening $200\text{ m}$, initial stage $660.0\text{ m}$ nominal with minor documented numerical adjustment to $659.643428\text{ m}$ to match baseline stored volume within $0.0000009\%$, roughness $n = 0.035$) are hypothetical research inputs.
2. **Three Distinct Resolution Levels**:
   - *Numerical Mesh Resolution*: Unstructured adaptive finite-volume elements ($\le 50\text{ m}$ breach zone, $\le 100\text{ m}$ corridor, $\le 200\text{ m}$ outer domain, $131,351$ triangles, $65,941$ vertices; measured edge lengths: min $28.91\text{ m}$, median $43.36\text{ m}$, $p_{95}$ $60.99\text{ m}$, max $93.75\text{ m}$; 11 crossing edges / 10 discrete intervals across 200 m breach opening).
   - *Exported Visualization Grid*: $50\text{ m}$ regular GeoTIFF raster arrays ($442 \times 600$, $265,200$ cells) in EPSG:32643 UTM Zone 43N. Outer domain areas are supported by $100\text{--}200\text{ m}$ mesh elements and contain nearest-neighbour and bilinear interpolation.
   - *Interpolated Display Rendering*: Smooth bilinear resampling for continuous depth/velocity with crisp transparent dry/NoData masks; nearest-neighbour for arrival time. High visual quality does **not** increase underlying physical accuracy.
3. **Volume-Matched Mesh Sensitivity (No Demonstrated Convergence)**:
   Differences between Phase 15 baseline ($200\text{ m}$ uniform mesh) and Phase 17 refined model (inundation IoU $= 0.7842$, inundated area $54.81\text{ km}^2$ vs $44.47\text{ km}^2$, depth MAE $= 0.9146\text{ m}$, depth RMSE $= 1.2766\text{ m}$, depth Mean Bias $= +0.0820\text{ m}$, velocity MAE $= 0.6291\text{ m/s}$, velocity RMSE $= 0.8249\text{ m/s}$, velocity Mean Bias $= +0.1385\text{ m/s}$) demonstrate **volume-matched mesh sensitivity; numerical convergence is not demonstrated**. Refined spatial discretization improves visual and numerical gradient representation without implying higher physical accuracy. Peak breach discharges (Baseline $\approx 9,794.37\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ vs Refined $\approx 21,953.43\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$) are approximate and not directly comparable due to 1-cell (one $200\text{ m}$ structured cross-mesh cell represented by four triangles and five vertices) vs 10-interval discretization. Exposure screening differences ($h \ge 0.10\text{ m}$): Assets exposed = **8** (Baseline) vs **62** (Refined), $\Delta = +54$; Screening-positive road segments (depth $\ge 0.10$ assumed metres) = **108** (Baseline) vs **128** (Refined), $\Delta = +20$. (At unthresholded $h > 0.00\text{ m}$, baseline exposes 29 assets due to 21 sub-threshold shallow assets with $0.02\text{--}0.09\text{ m}$ depth).
4. **Boundary Integrity, Leakage & Mass Balance**:
   Dam embankment outside the $200\text{ m}$ breach is modeled as a continuous raised impermeable crest ($675.0\text{ m}$ elevation). Non-breach instantaneous leakage rate is $0.0\text{ assumed m}^3/\text{s}$ and cumulative non-breach leakage is $0.0\text{ assumed m}^3$ via trapezoidal time-integration. Mass balance is preserved under finite-volume continuity ($\text{initial storage} = \text{final storage} + \text{cumulative boundary outflow} = 317,161,857.3221\text{ m}^3$, relative error $2.16 \times 10^{-14}$).
